04 · Operational thinking

Process Improvement Case

An end-to-end example of how I would diagnose an operational bottleneck, design a practical control and measure whether it improves delivery.

The scenario · illustrative

Requests are getting lost between inboxes, chat threads and verbal follow-ups.

A small team is missing internal deadlines. People are busy, but there is no single view of what has been requested, who owns it, when it is due or what is blocking it.

Symptoms observed
  • Requests arrive through several channels.
  • Important details are missing at the point of handover.
  • Ownership is unclear after a request is received.
  • Follow-ups depend on individual memory.
  • Leaders discover delays late.
Likely process causes
  • No standard intake fields or request log.
  • No explicit accountable owner or due date.
  • No agreed status definitions.
  • No routine review of blockers and overdue items.
  • No clear escalation path.
Proposed future workflow

01 · Capture

Log every request in one shared register with requestor, required outcome, priority and date received.

02 · Assign

Confirm one accountable owner, a due date and any approval or information dependencies.

03 · Track

Use a consistent status: Not started, In progress, At risk, Blocked or Done. Record the next action.

04 · Review

Hold a short weekly review to resolve blockers, reset priorities and confirm decisions.

05 · Close

Confirm completion, store the final output in the agreed location and record any handover.

Measures I would monitor
MeasureWhat it tells the teamWhen to baseline
On-time completion rateWhether work is delivered by its agreed due dateBefore the new workflow
Overdue task countHow much work is past due and still openWeekly
Age of unresolved blockersHow long dependencies prevent progressAt each review
Incomplete request rateHow often work is delayed by missing informationMonthly
Document retrieval timeHow quickly staff can locate the current approved fileBefore and after change
Implementation plan
  1. Week 1: Map current request channels and agree minimum intake information.
  2. Week 2: Set up the shared register, status definitions and ownership rules.
  3. Week 3: Pilot with one team or workstream; gather feedback and refine.
  4. Week 4: Review the first measures, adjust the process and confirm ongoing ownership.
